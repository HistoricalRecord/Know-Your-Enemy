1948 declaration / war / displacement

Israel declared independence on May 14 1948. Fighting followed between Israel and neighboring Arab states.

The war produced major population displacement. Palestinians refer to the mass displacement as the Nakba. Israeli historical memory places the war within the struggle for Jewish national independence and survival.

The armistice agreements of 1949 established the lines that became known as the Green Line.

One declaration. Two national memories. One war. A map that never stopped moving.

1956 Suez Crisis

Israel invaded Egypt in October 1956 in coordination with Britain and France during the Suez Crisis.

The crisis followed Egyptian President Gamal Abdel Nasser's nationalization of the Suez Canal.

Pressure from the United States and Soviet Union contributed to the withdrawal of the invading forces.

1967 six days

In June 1967 Israel fought Egypt Jordan and Syria.

Israel captured the Sinai Peninsula Gaza Strip West Bank East Jerusalem and Golan Heights.

The war transformed the political geography of the region. Questions surrounding occupation settlements borders Palestinian statehood Jerusalem and security remain connected to the consequences of 1967.

Six days. Decades afterward.

1973 Yom Kippur War

Egypt and Syria attacked Israeli positions on October 6 1973.

The date coincided with Yom Kippur the holiest day in the Jewish calendar. It also occurred during Ramadan.

The United States provided military support to Israel. The Soviet Union supported Arab states.

The conflict contributed to later diplomatic negotiations between Egypt and Israel.

1982 Lebanon

Israel invaded Lebanon in June 1982. The operation was initially presented as an effort to stop attacks by the Palestine Liberation Organization.

Israeli forces reached Beirut. The PLO leadership eventually left Lebanon.

The Sabra and Shatila massacre occurred in September 1982 in camps in Beirut. Lebanese Christian Phalangist forces carried out the killings. An Israeli commission later found Israeli personnel responsible for failing to prevent the massacre and concluded that senior Israeli officials bore indirect responsibility.

GAZA occupation / withdrawal / blockade / war

Israel captured Gaza in 1967.

Israel removed its settlements and permanent ground forces from Gaza in 2005.

Hamas won Palestinian legislative elections in 2006 and took control of Gaza in 2007 after armed conflict with Fatah.

Israel and Egypt subsequently imposed restrictions on movement into and out of Gaza.

Gaza has experienced repeated major armed conflicts involving Israel and Hamas.

The October 7 2023 Hamas-led attack on Israel killed approximately 1,200 people according to Israeli authorities and resulted in the taking of hostages.

Israel subsequently launched a large military campaign in Gaza.

The war produced extensive Palestinian civilian casualties displacement and destruction and generated international legal proceedings and allegations concerning the conduct of the conflict.

JERUSALEM city / temple / mosque / state / memory

Jerusalem is sacred to Judaism Christianity and Islam.

The ancient Jewish Temples stood on the Temple Mount. The Western Wall remains one of the most important Jewish places of prayer.

The site is also associated with the Dome of the Rock and Al-Aqsa Mosque.

Modern sovereignty and access arrangements surrounding Jerusalem remain among the most contested questions in the Israeli-Palestinian conflict.

JEWISH TEXTS & TRADITIONS Torah / Temple / exile / return

Jewish religious history contains traditions concerning covenant exile return Jerusalem sacrifice repentance and messianic expectation.

Yom Kippur is the Day of Atonement. Leviticus 16 describes an ancient ritual involving two goats. One was sacrificed while another was sent into the wilderness after the community's sins were symbolically placed upon it.

The English word scapegoat developed from this tradition.

These traditions belong to Jewish religious history. They do not establish modern political claims.

THEORIES claims / evidence / contradiction

Some conspiracy claims concerning Israel and Jews allege secret control of governments banking media wars intelligence agencies or international institutions.

Similar claims have appeared for centuries in different forms. Some derive from older antisemitic myths such as the fabricated Protocols of the Elders of Zion.

Other claims concern specific Israeli governments intelligence operations military decisions lobbying organizations or documented political relationships.

Those categories should not be treated as interchangeable. A specific documented action by an Israeli institution is evidence about that institution. It is not evidence that Jews as a group secretly control events.

A coincidence is not a connection. A connection is not a conspiracy. A conspiracy requires evidence of an agreement or coordinated action.
