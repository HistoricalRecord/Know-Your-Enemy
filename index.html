1948 / 1956 / 1967 / 1973 / 1982 / 1993 / 2005 / 2007 / 2023

Israel was established in 1948 after the United Nations General Assembly had adopted a partition proposal for Mandatory Palestine.

The state that emerged from the war expanded beyond the territory assigned to the proposed Jewish state under Resolution 181.

The wars that followed changed borders populations alliances military doctrines diplomatic relationships and the meaning of territory throughout the region.

The record below follows those changes through governments military operations diplomatic agreements intelligence activity political organizations religious history and primary documents.

1947–1949 partition / independence / war / displacement
NOVEMBER 29 1947

Resolution 181

The United Nations General Assembly adopted Resolution 181 recommending partition of Mandatory Palestine into an Arab state and a Jewish state, with Jerusalem under a special international regime.

The proposal was accepted by the Jewish Agency and rejected by Arab leaders and the Arab states.

Civil conflict followed between Jewish and Arab communities. The British Mandate ended in May 1948.

On May 14 1948 David Ben-Gurion declared the establishment of the State of Israel.

Egypt, Transjordan, Syria, Lebanon and Iraq subsequently entered the war.

By the time armistice agreements were reached in 1949, Israel controlled substantially more territory than the Jewish state proposed by Resolution 181.

The West Bank came under Jordanian control and Gaza came under Egyptian administration.

Hundreds of thousands of Palestinians became refugees. The causes and circumstances of individual departures varied and remain heavily contested in historical scholarship.

1949

The Armistice Lines

Israel signed armistice agreements with Egypt Lebanon Jordan and Syria during 1949.

These agreements established ceasefire lines rather than permanent international borders.

The line separating Israel from the West Bank became known as the Green Line.

Jerusalem remained divided between Israeli-controlled West Jerusalem and Jordanian-controlled East Jerusalem.

1956 Suez / Sinai / Britain / France / Israel / Egypt
JULY — NOVEMBER 1956

The Suez Crisis

Egyptian President Gamal Abdel Nasser nationalized the Suez Canal Company on July 26 1956.

Britain and France opposed the nationalization. Israel regarded Nasser's Egypt as a security threat and had been engaged in cross-border conflict with Egypt.

British French and Israeli officials secretly negotiated at Sèvres near Paris.

The plan called for Israel to invade Sinai. Britain and France would then issue an ultimatum to Israel and Egypt and intervene militarily around the canal.

Israel invaded Sinai on October 29. Britain and France subsequently attacked Egypt.

The Eisenhower administration opposed the operation and pressured Britain and France to accept a ceasefire.

The United States therefore found itself opposing the actions of two European allies and Israel during the crisis.

1967 Egypt / Jordan / Syria / Sinai / West Bank / Gaza / Golan
JUNE 5 — 10 1967

Six Days

Tensions between Israel and Arab states escalated during May and early June 1967.

Egypt moved forces into Sinai and demanded the withdrawal of United Nations peacekeepers.

Egypt also closed the Straits of Tiran to Israeli shipping.

Israel launched a large-scale air attack against Egypt on June 5.

Jordan and Syria subsequently entered the fighting.

Israel captured the Sinai Peninsula and Gaza Strip from Egypt, the West Bank and East Jerusalem from Jordan, and the Golan Heights from Syria.

The war transformed the geography of the region.

The occupation of the territories captured in 1967 became the central issue of subsequent Israeli-Palestinian diplomacy.

SIX DAYS CHANGED THE MAP

NOVEMBER 22 1967

Resolution 242

The United Nations Security Council adopted Resolution 242 following the war.

The resolution established principles for a settlement involving withdrawal of Israeli forces from territories occupied in the conflict and recognition of every state's right to live in peace within secure and recognized boundaries.

The wording became central to subsequent diplomacy.

United Nations — History of the Question of Palestine
1973 Yom Kippur / Ramadan / Egypt / Syria / United States / USSR
OCTOBER 6 1973

Yom Kippur

Egypt and Syria launched a coordinated attack against Israeli forces on October 6 1973.

The attack coincided with Yom Kippur, the Jewish Day of Atonement, and occurred during Ramadan.

Egypt crossed the Suez Canal into the Sinai Peninsula. Syrian forces attacked across the Golan Heights.

Israel initially suffered major battlefield setbacks.

The United States subsequently launched a major airlift of military equipment to Israel.

The Soviet Union supplied Egypt and Syria.

The war ended after a United Nations ceasefire resolution and subsequent negotiations.

The conflict contributed to the diplomatic process that eventually produced the Egypt-Israel peace treaty.

1982 Lebanon / Beirut / PLO / Sabra and Shatila
JUNE 1982

Lebanon

Israel invaded Lebanon in June 1982 following escalating violence along the Israel-Lebanon border and an attempted assassination of Israel's ambassador in London.

The stated Israeli objective was to push PLO forces away from Israel's northern border.

Israeli forces advanced north toward Beirut.

The Palestine Liberation Organization leadership eventually evacuated Lebanon under an internationally negotiated arrangement.

The war produced extensive civilian casualties and international controversy.

SEPTEMBER 1982

Sabra and Shatila

Fighters from the Lebanese Christian Phalange entered the Sabra and Shatila refugee camps in Beirut after the departure of PLO forces.

Large numbers of civilians were killed.

An Israeli commission of inquiry, the Kahan Commission, investigated the massacre.

The commission concluded that the direct perpetrators were Lebanese Phalangists and found indirect responsibility among Israeli officials for failing to take appropriate steps to prevent the massacre.

THE QUESTION WAS NOT WHETHER THE MASSACRE OCCURRED. THE QUESTION WAS WHO KNEW WHAT WHEN.

1993 Oslo / PLO / Israel / recognition
SEPTEMBER 1993

Oslo

Israel and the Palestine Liberation Organization signed the Declaration of Principles on Interim Self-Government Arrangements in September 1993.

Israeli Prime Minister Yitzhak Rabin and PLO Chairman Yasser Arafat formally recognized each other.

The agreement created a framework for limited Palestinian self-government and further negotiations.

The Oslo process did not resolve the questions of borders, Jerusalem, refugees, settlements or permanent sovereignty.

The process subsequently deteriorated amid violence, political opposition and the assassination of Rabin in 1995.

Source
U.S. Department of State — Oslo Accords
2005 Gaza disengagement
AUGUST — SEPTEMBER 2005

Disengagement

Israel removed its settlements and permanent ground forces from the Gaza Strip in 2005.

Israeli forces continued to control or influence Gaza's external borders airspace and maritime access in varying forms after the withdrawal.

The meaning of the withdrawal became a major point of disagreement in subsequent legal and political debates concerning the status of Gaza.

2006–2007 Hamas / elections / Fatah / Gaza

Hamas won a majority in the Palestinian legislative elections held in January 2006.

Political conflict between Hamas and Fatah escalated into armed fighting.

Hamas took control of Gaza in June 2007.

The Palestinian political system subsequently became divided between the Hamas-controlled Gaza Strip and the Palestinian Authority areas of the West Bank.

Israel and Egypt imposed significant restrictions on movement and goods entering and leaving Gaza.

The resulting political division became a central feature of Israeli-Palestinian politics.

GAZA 2008 / 2012 / 2014 / 2021 / 2023–

Repeated Wars

Gaza experienced repeated major rounds of armed conflict involving Israel and Hamas and other Palestinian armed groups.

Major escalations occurred in 2008–2009 2012 2014 2021 and following October 7 2023.

Rocket attacks from Gaza targeted Israeli territory. Israeli military operations struck Gaza using air ground and naval forces.

Civilian casualties and destruction occurred on both sides, with the scale of destruction in Gaza becoming particularly extensive during the war beginning in October 2023.

OCTOBER 7 2023

The Attack

Hamas-led Palestinian armed groups attacked communities and military positions in southern Israel.

Approximately 1,200 people were killed according to Israeli authorities and approximately 250 people were taken hostage.

Israel subsequently declared war and launched a major military campaign against Hamas in Gaza.

The campaign produced enormous civilian destruction displacement and casualties.

The conflict also produced major international legal proceedings.

2024

International Court of Justice

South Africa brought proceedings against Israel before the International Court of Justice under the Genocide Convention.

In January 2024 the Court ordered provisional measures requiring Israel to take measures within its power to prevent acts falling within Article II of the Genocide Convention and to preserve evidence relevant to the case.

The Court later reaffirmed and modified provisional measures during 2024.

Provisional measures are not a final judgment on the merits.

JERUSALEM temple / wall / mosque / kingdom / city

A City With Several Histories

Jerusalem has religious significance in Judaism Christianity and Islam.

Ancient Jewish tradition connects Jerusalem with the Davidic kingdom and Solomon's Temple.

The First Temple is traditionally associated with Solomon. The Second Temple was rebuilt following the Babylonian exile and later greatly expanded under Herod.

Roman forces destroyed the Second Temple in 70 CE.

The Western Wall is a surviving retaining wall associated with the Temple complex and became a major Jewish site of prayer and pilgrimage.

The Temple Mount is also the site of the Dome of the Rock and Al-Aqsa Mosque.

Control access archaeology excavation prayer and sovereignty at the site remain politically sensitive.

1967

Israel captured East Jerusalem from Jordan during the Six Day War.

Israel subsequently extended its law administration and jurisdiction to East Jerusalem.

Israel considers Jerusalem its capital. Most states historically maintained embassies elsewhere because of the disputed status of the city.

The United States recognized Jerusalem as Israel's capital in 2017 and moved its embassy there in 2018.

SINAI 1956 / 1967 / 1979 / withdrawal

Israel occupied Sinai during the Suez Crisis of 1956.

Israel captured Sinai again during the 1967 war.

Egypt and Israel signed the Camp David Accords in 1978.

The Egypt-Israel Peace Treaty followed in 1979.

Israel completed its withdrawal from Sinai in 1982.

Territory captured in 1967. Territory returned through treaty. A different outcome from the West Bank.

WEST BANK 1967 / occupation / settlements / Oslo

Israel captured the West Bank from Jordan in 1967.

Israeli settlements were subsequently established throughout parts of the territory.

The Oslo process divided the West Bank into Areas A B and C with differing Palestinian and Israeli administrative and security responsibilities.

Area C remains under Israeli civil and security control.

The status of settlements is disputed internationally. Israel has disputed interpretations that describe all settlement activity as unlawful.

LEBANON 1978 / 1982 / 2000 / 2006

Israel entered southern Lebanon during Operation Litani in 1978.

Israel invaded Lebanon in 1982 and maintained a security zone in southern Lebanon for years afterward.

Israel withdrew from southern Lebanon in 2000.

Hezbollah and Israel fought a major war in 2006.

The 2006 conflict involved extensive Israeli air operations and ground fighting and large-scale Hezbollah rocket attacks against northern Israel.

GOLAN HEIGHTS Syria / Israel / strategic territory

Israel captured the Golan Heights from Syria during the June 1967 war.

Israel effectively extended its administration to the area in 1981.

The United Nations Security Council declared the Israeli action without international legal effect.

The United States recognized Israeli sovereignty over the Golan Heights in 2019.

Most of the international community has not recognized Israeli sovereignty over the territory.

WASHINGTON military assistance / diplomacy / intelligence / politics

The Alliance

The United States and Israel have maintained a close strategic relationship for decades.

The relationship includes military assistance weapons sales missile-defense cooperation intelligence cooperation diplomatic coordination and joint strategic programs.

U.S. policy has changed across administrations.

The United States opposed the British French and Israeli military operation during the Suez Crisis in 1956.

By 1973 the United States was conducting a major military airlift supplying Israel during the Yom Kippur War.

The relationship therefore cannot be reduced to a single uninterrupted policy beginning in 1948.

Political Influence

American organizations supporting Israel participate openly in U.S. political life through lobbying campaign contributions public advocacy coalition building and policy research.

Political influence can be studied through campaign finance records lobbying disclosures congressional votes executive decisions and public statements.

Those records concern identifiable organizations and people. They do not establish that American Jews as a group control the U.S. government.

INTELLIGENCE Mossad / Shin Bet / Military Intelligence / covert operations

Three Systems

Israel maintains several major intelligence organizations.

Organization General Function
Mossad Intelligence collection and covert activity primarily outside Israel.
Shin Bet Internal security and counterintelligence.
Military Intelligence Military intelligence collection analysis and warning.

Intelligence organizations operate in secrecy by design. Public records therefore tend to consist of declassified documents official statements investigative reporting court records memoirs and later disclosures.

Intelligence Failure

The 1973 war demonstrated that intelligence services can possess substantial information and still misinterpret an adversary's intentions.

U.S. intelligence assessments before the war also underestimated the likelihood of an Egyptian-Syrian attack.

Intelligence failure is therefore not synonymous with absence of intelligence.

The question is what was collected what was believed what was communicated and what decision-makers did with it.

JEWISH RELIGION Yom Kippur / atonement / scapegoat / Temple

Yom Kippur

Yom Kippur is the Jewish Day of Atonement and is the culmination of the High Holy Days.

The biblical description of the ancient Yom Kippur ritual appears in Leviticus 16.

Two goats were selected.

One was designated as an offering. The other became associated with the ritual later known through English as the scapegoat.

The high priest symbolically placed the sins of the community upon the second goat and sent it into the wilderness.

The modern English word scapegoat developed through translations of this passage.

Temple

The ancient Jerusalem Temple occupied the center of Jewish sacrificial worship.

The destruction of the Second Temple by Rome in 70 CE fundamentally changed Jewish religious life.

Rabbinic Judaism developed without the Temple sacrificial system and placed greater emphasis on prayer Torah study repentance charity and communal religious practice.

The Temple remains central to Jewish historical memory and to religious traditions concerning Jerusalem.

1903 Protocols of the Elders of Zion
RUSSIAN EMPIRE

The Forged Document

The Protocols of the Elders of Zion was published in Russia beginning in 1903.

It purported to record secret meetings of Jewish leaders planning to control governments financial systems media education and international affairs.

The purported meetings never occurred.

Investigations established that substantial portions of the text were plagiarized from earlier works of political satire including Maurice Joly's 1864 Dialogue in Hell Between Machiavelli and Montesquieu.

The document nevertheless became one of the most widely circulated antisemitic texts of the twentieth century.

Its importance to the historical record is not that the conspiracy described in it occurred.

Its importance is that a fabricated document was repeatedly treated as evidence of a nonexistent international conspiracy.

A DOCUMENT CAN EXIST WITHOUT THE EVENTS DESCRIBED IN THE DOCUMENT EVER HAVING OCCURRED.

CLAIMS names / documents / operations / conclusions

Where the Record Becomes Difficult

There are documented Israeli military operations.

There are documented intelligence operations.

There are documented covert agreements.

There are documented political relationships between Israel and the United States.

There are documented lobbying organizations.

There are documented cases involving Israeli officials intelligence personnel military personnel and companies.

There are documented wars and documented civilian deaths.

There are also claims that extend beyond those records.

The difference matters.

The Suez Crisis provides an example of a genuine secret agreement involving Israel Britain and France.

The existence of that agreement does not establish that every later theory about secret Israeli control of unrelated governments is true.

The distinction is not about whether governments conduct secret operations.

They do.

The distinction is whether the particular claim has evidence behind it.
